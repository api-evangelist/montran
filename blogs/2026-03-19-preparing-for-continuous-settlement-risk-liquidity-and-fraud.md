<h3><strong>Introduction</strong></h3>
<p>As the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) advances its vision of a continuously operating financial infrastructure, the Philippine payment ecosystem is entering a new phase of real-time activity. The evolution toward extended settlement windows—eventually supporting near 24/7 operations—represents a structural shift for banks, payment service providers, and market infrastructures.</p>
<h3><strong>Questions Banks Should Ask Today</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Can our payment infrastructure support continuous RTGS connectivity?</li>
<li>Do we have real-time liquidity visibility across payment rails?</li>
<li>How quickly can our systems detect fraud in high-value payments?</li>
<li>Are our payment platforms prepared for multi-lateral cross-border instant payment interoperability?</li>
</ul>

<h3>The Risk Multiplier of Continuous Settlement<!-- notionvc: b88db323-3567-4bc9-a51f-c69bbfe147f4 --><br />
<!-- notionvc: bb3a62d3-eee6-40eb-a1c4-9cdba0c66260 --></h3>
<p>In the Philippines, PhilPaSSplus is the backbone of peso settlement in central bank money. It supports interbank transfers, securities settlement, and ultimately underpins the retail payment ecosystem.</p>
<blockquote><p><strong>That ecosystem is expanding rapidly.</strong></p></blockquote>
<p>In 2023 alone, InstaPay and PESONet processed ₱12.8 trillion (USD 228 billion) across more than 929 million transactions. By 2025, combined values reached approximately ₱24.7 trillion (USD 440 billion) with 4.8 billion transactions. Digital payments now account for more than half of all retail transactions in the Philippines.</p>
<p>Much of this value ultimately depends on final settlement in PhilPaSSplus.</p>
<p>Extending RTGS hours strengthens liquidity and cross-border alignment, consistent with global trends under the G20 roadmap for faster, cheaper, safer and more transparent payments. However, it also amplifies several risk dimensions:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Fraud velocity increases. </strong>Continuous Real-time settlement compresses response windows from hours to seconds.</li>
<li><strong>Operational fatigue intensifies. </strong>Continuous systems reduce traditional pause points for reconciliation and review.</li>
<li><strong>Cross-channel exposure expands. </strong>Retail-originated fraud can rapidly propagate toward high-value settlement rails.</li>
<li><strong>Liquidity impact accelerates. </strong>Fraud events in a 24/7 environment can quickly affect intraday and overnight positions.</li>
</ul>
<p><span id="more-3608"></span></p>
<p>These risks are not theoretical. BSP-supervised institutions reported ₱5.82 billion (USD 104 million) in cyber-related losses in 2024, slightly up from ₱5.67 billion (USD 101 million) in 2023. Phishing and card-not-present fraud alone accounted for over ₱3.3 billion (USD 59 million). Cybercrime complaints to authorities tripled year-on-year in 2024.</p>
<p>The Philippines also recorded one of the highest suspected digital fraud rates globally in 2024, at 13.4% of digital transactions—well above the global average. As digital adoption rises, so does the attack surface.</p>
<blockquote><p><strong>In a 24/7 RTGS environment, these pressures intensify.</strong></p></blockquote>

<h3><span class="notion-enable-hover">Liquidity Becomes a 24/7 Discipline</span><!-- notionvc: b896ee22-fde2-4fe0-a226-a4b411fcadff --><br />
<!-- notionvc: f758a4c6-0986-4b82-b6bf-bf127ddb7f6b --></h3>
<p>Continuous settlement fundamentally changes how banks manage liquidity.</p>
<p>Historically, RTGS systems operated within defined windows that allowed institutions to forecast and rebalance positions during the day. As settlement hours expand, liquidity management becomes a continuous discipline.</p>
<p>Banks must coordinate liquidity across multiple payment rails simultaneously:</p>
<ul>
<li>RTGS settlement systems</li>
<li>Instant payment platforms such as InstaPay</li>
<li>Batch clearing systems such as PESONet</li>
<li>Cross-border payment corridors</li>
</ul>
<p><!--more--></p>
<p>In a 24/7 environment, delays in liquidity allocation can cascade across payment channels. Institutions increasingly require real-time visibility into balances, settlement queues, and liquidity usage across infrastructures.</p>
<blockquote><p><strong>This shift is transforming liquidity management from a treasury function into a real-time operational capability.</strong></p></blockquote>

<p><!-- notionvc: 2dc52e40-7499-4272-8f64-fc4c9c8e2da7 --></p>
<p><!--more--><!-- notionvc: 556864ff-c387-4fe5-ba4c-55611eeabe1b --></p>
<p><!-- notionvc: bd77b3a1-61ad-4a31-bc0e-94c4d630d7f3 --></p>
<h3><span class="notion-enable-hover">Cross-Border Interoperability Raises the Stakes</span><!-- notionvc: 805c3146-82ff-4768-98b9-c1a234d1c2c9 --><br />
<!-- notionvc: 58d2e257-933f-4f87-84c0-5beb6fe27687 --></h3>
<p>The Philippines is among the world’s largest recipients of remittances, with cross-border flows representing a critical component of the national economy.</p>
<p>As central banks pursue the G20 roadmap for faster and more transparent cross-border payments, the interoperability between domestic infrastructures and international payment networks becomes increasingly important.</p>
<p>Extended settlement windows support this evolution by aligning operating hours across jurisdictions. However, they also introduce new operational and risk management requirements.</p>
<p>Fraud monitoring, liquidity visibility, and payment orchestration must operate seamlessly across domestic and international payment rails.</p>
<blockquote><p><strong>Institutions that can unify these capabilities gain greater resilience as payment ecosystems become globally interconnected.</strong></p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="notion-enable-hover">Regulation Has Already Raised the Baseline</span><!-- notionvc: 9ecd1419-2733-4627-9642-791f6849a87f --></h3>
<p><!-- notionvc: a8f1bbac-a201-4c64-8128-88c1dfcc5b4a --></p>
<p>BSP Circular No. 1140 requires supervised institutions to deploy automated and real-time fraud monitoring and detection systems and to integrate these with anti-money laundering (AML) frameworks into a cohesive financial crime prevention architecture.</p>
<p>In other words, real-time fraud monitoring is no longer aspirational—it is a regulatory expectation.</p>
<p>As operating hours extend, compliance with these standards becomes structurally more complex. Manual reviews, batch alerts, and static rule engines are not designed for uninterrupted settlement cycles.</p>
<blockquote><p><strong>A 24/7 RTGS system demands 24/7 intelligence.</strong></p></blockquote>
<p><!-- notionvc: 0e4d13da-1982-4b8d-a88c-4b8f953ac2ff --><!--more--></p>
<h3><span class="notion-enable-hover">AI as Embedded Infrastructure</span><!-- notionvc: 321ecf44-57d7-4a7a-b0a7-80eb6f2265cb --><br />
<!-- notionvc: e4894258-7c4d-4b95-a43e-147f43bca2f2 --></h3>
<p>Artificial intelligence is often described as an innovation layer. In continuous settlement environments, it becomes foundational.</p>
<p>Threat actors are already using AI to scale phishing campaigns, enhance identity takeover attempts, and automate fraud typologies. Defensive systems must evolve accordingly.</p>
<p>AI-driven fraud prevention capabilities can:</p>
<ul>
<li>Detect behavioral anomalies across participants and counterparties in real time</li>
<li>Adapt dynamically to emerging fraud patterns</li>
<li>Correlate liquidity movements with suspicious transactional behavior</li>
<li>Reduce false positives while accelerating high-confidence intervention</li>
<li>Integrate fraud and AML signals into a unified monitoring framework</li>
</ul>
<p><!--more--></p>
<p>In a continuous settlement model, static controls introduce latency. Latency introduces exposure. <strong>AI-enabled systems reduce latency.</strong></p>
<blockquote><p><strong>When embedded directly within RTGS and instant payment infrastructures—not bolted on as an afterthought—advanced analytics transform risk management from reactive investigation to predictive protection.</strong></p></blockquote>

<h3><span class="notion-enable-hover">Integration Is the Strategic Imperative</span></h3>
<p>Extended operating hours does not merely require stronger fraud tools—it requires integrated financial crime frameworks that unify detection, prevention, and response across all channels. Siloed systems fragment insight; integrated systems strengthen resilience.<!-- notionvc: 1cbf082e-5d97-4a6a-836e-1ccd3e36343c --></p>
<p>An effective 24/7 architecture should:</p>
<ul>
<li>Connect RTGS, instant payment rails, and centralized fraud monitoring systems</li>
<li>Share intelligence across channels and counterparties</li>
<li>Align fraud detection with AML processes and liquidity monitoring</li>
<li>Provide real-time executive visibility into systemic risk exposure</li>
</ul>
<blockquote><p><strong>BSP’s regulatory stance already points toward this model: fraud monitoring and AML systems must operate cohesively. In a 24/7 RTGS environment, fragmentation becomes a systemic vulnerability.</strong><!-- notionvc: b69c195e-2954-489a-87d2-387be41261d7 --></p></blockquote>

<h3>RTGS in an Always-On Payment Ecosystem<!-- notionvc: 3622aefb-fb83-4f0a-926f-a6839f0fdc3c --></h3>
<p>While most instant payment systems already operate on a 24/7 basis, the wholesale settlement infrastructures that underpin national financial systems have historically operated within defined daytime windows.</p>
<blockquote><p><strong>This creates an increasingly important architectural challenge.</strong></p></blockquote>
<p>Retail payment systems, digital commerce platforms, and cross-border payment corridors now function continuously. Yet many RTGS systems—the ultimate source of settlement finality in central bank money—still operate on limited schedules.</p>
<p>Central banks around the world are therefore evaluating how wholesale settlement infrastructures should evolve to support an always-on financial ecosystem.</p>
<p>Several approaches are emerging:</p>
<ul>
<li>Extended RTGS operating hours, allowing greater overlap with global markets</li>
<li>Liquidity bridges between RTGS systems and instant payment infrastructures</li>
<li>Prefunding models to support continuous retail payment settlement</li>
<li>Interoperability frameworks linking domestic infrastructures with cross-border payment networks</li>
</ul>
<p><!--more--></p>
<p>In this context, the Philippines’ exploration of extended PhilPaSSplus operating hours reflects a broader global challenge: ensuring that core settlement infrastructures remain aligned with payment ecosystems that increasingly operate without interruption.</p>
<p>The transition is not simply about extending system availability. It requires rethinking how liquidity, risk monitoring, and payment orchestration operate across interconnected payment rails.</p>
<blockquote><p><strong>Financial infrastructure never sleeps—and risk management must evolve accordingly.</strong></p></blockquote>
<p><!-- notionvc: f8c4753e-7277-4eef-bfd6-4776df23232c --></p>

<h3>A Strategic Inflection Point for the Philippines<!-- notionvc: 916e737f-bfa6-4e15-9819-43567115de2e --><br />
<!-- notionvc: 3622aefb-fb83-4f0a-926f-a6839f0fdc3c --></h3>
<p>As the Philippine payment ecosystem evolves toward continuous settlement, institutions must rethink how risk management, liquidity monitoring, and payment orchestration operate in real time.</p>
<p>Modern financial infrastructures increasingly embed advanced analytics, intelligent monitoring, and unified liquidity visibility directly within payment systems rather than relying on disconnected controls.</p>
<ul>
<li>As settlement infrastructures expand operating hours, banks increasingly require unified platforms that provide real-time visibility across RTGS systems, instant payment networks, and cross-border payment channels.</li>
<li>For participating institutions, extended RTGS operations will also reshape treasury and liquidity management practices, requiring banks to maintain real-time visibility across settlement positions throughout the day and night.</li>
</ul>
